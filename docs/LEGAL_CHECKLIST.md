# Legal & Launch Checklist — "Cover Your Own Ass"

> I'm an engineer, not a lawyer. This is practical guidance assembled from how the site actually
> works, not legal advice. Before you take real money, have it reviewed by a professional familiar
> with your jurisdiction.

The single biggest exposure with a site like this is that you collect **other people's personal
data** (recipient names, custom messages) and **publish some of it publicly**. Everything below
flows from that.

---

## A. Business & payments

- [ ] **Registered business or sole trader?** Decide before launch — payment providers and tax
      authorities want to know who is selling.
- [ ] **Stripe account is in the right legal name** and matches your site's Contact page.
- [ ] **Contact page has a real mailing address.** Many consumer-protection laws (and Stripe's own
      requirements) expect a physical/contact address and legal entity name. The template has
      placeholders — fill them in.
- [ ] **Tax.** Sales tax / VAT rules for digital goods vary by buyer location. Talk to an accountant.
      Stripe Tax can automate collection if you enable it.
- [ ] **Refund policy is real and matches the code.** The webhook marks orders `paid`; refunds happen
      in the Stripe dashboard. Keep `/refunds` consistent with what you actually do.

## B. Privacy (the big one)

- [ ] **Privacy Policy** matches reality. It names the processors (Stripe, Supabase, Resend, host).
      If you add analytics or a newsletter later, update it.
- [ ] **Data minimization.** You collect recipient names + messages. Only keep what you need, and
      define a retention window (e.g. delete private dedications after N months).
- [ ] **GDPR/CCPA deletion path.** If a recipient (who never visited your site) emails "delete my
      name," you need a way to find and remove dedications by name. Add an admin step for this.
- [ ] **Public feed consent.** The feed only shows entries where `is_public = true`. You already
      default it to on with a visible checkbox — consider whether the *recipient's* name should be
      shown there too, or only the dedicator's. Showing a non-consenting third party's name publicly
      is the sharpest edge here.
- [ ] **Cookie banner?** You set no marketing cookies today (only fonts + Supabase session-less
      reads), so a banner is likely unnecessary — but re-check if you add analytics.
- [ ] **Children.** Site isn't directed at under-13s; the policy says so.

## C. Content & IP

- [ ] **KJV text is public domain** in the US/UK (published 1611). Safe to reproduce.
- [ ] **Verse references / book names** are used descriptively — fine.
- [ ] **User-submitted messages.** Terms say you may remove unlawful content. Add a way to actually
      do that (admin delete) before you rely on it.
- [ ] **Have a moderation plan** for the public feed. Even with a small site, one hateful "message"
      in the feed is a reputational and possibly legal problem.
- [ ] **Fonts** (Cormorant Garamond, Inter) are Google Fonts — open licensed, fine.
- [ ] **Images.** None are bundled. If you add stock imagery, license it (don't scrape).

## D. The "10% to Bible translation" claim

This deserves special care because it's a **charitable-sounding promise**:

- [ ] **Name the recipient.** "Supports Bible translation" is vague; a specific organization (with
      their permission) is defensible. Pick one and say who.
- [ ] **State it's not tax-deductible for the buyer** (already in the copy) — you are making a
      business contribution, not processing a donation.
- [ ] **Keep records** of each contribution so the claim is provable.
- [ ] **Don't call it a "tithe."** The old copy did; tithes are given to God, not generated by a
      sale, and the term can invite scrutiny. It's now "10% given."
- [ ] If you ever want to make it a formal donation program, that changes tax/legal treatment —
      get advice first.

## E. Accessibility & SEO

- [ ] Dialogs, filters, and FAQ are keyboard-accessible (done in code).
- [ ] Run Lighthouse / axe on the live site and fix anything flagged.
- [ ] Add `public/og-image.png` (1200×630) so shares look right.
- [ ] Confirm `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` resolve on the live domain.

## F. Operations

- [ ] **Backups.** Supabase has daily backups on paid tiers; otherwise export regularly.
- [ ] **Failure mode:** if PDF generation fails, the order is still `paid` and recorded. Set up a way
      to notice and regenerate (see README "not yet implemented").
- [ ] **Secret hygiene.** `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` and `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` live only in env vars,
      never in the repo. Rotate if leaked.
- [ ] **Rate limit** the checkout endpoint if you see abuse.
- [ ] **Monitor** the Stripe webhook dashboard for failed deliveries.

---

## Minimum to launch responsibly

1. Put a **real business name, address, and contact email** on the site.
2. Have someone review **Privacy + Terms + Refunds** for your jurisdiction.
3. Decide and document **who receives the 10%**, and track it.
4. Add an **admin delete** for public dedications so you can honor takedown requests.
5. Place one **live test order** end-to-end before announcing.
